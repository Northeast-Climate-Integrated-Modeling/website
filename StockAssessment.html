Stock Assessment

Overview

Our team has made significant progress developing climate-informed stock assessment models for species that have demonstrated shifts in distribution and changes in productivity, including American plaice, Atlantic cod, and black sea bass. In Phase II of NCLIM, we plan to continue our active participation in research track stock assessments and commitment to providing support in the integration of climate and ecosystem information.

Key Objective

Integration of climate impacts on fish stock dynamics to improve stock assessment

Current Initiatives

  • Stakeholder Engagement: Engage with fishers and fisheries stakeholders in the region with the aim of characterizing their observations of changes in the ecosystem and climate, fish distributions, or other relevant concerns.
  • Synthesis Work: Conduct synthesis to characterize the state of knowledge on ecosystem and climate impacts on fish stock dynamics.
  • Adaptation of a Flexible Assessment Model: the Woods Hole Assessment Model (WHAM) uses next-generation statistical methods to consider time varying processes and environmental covariates.
  • Accounting for Non-stationarity in Stock Dynamics: A step forward from assumptions of stationarity to acknowledgement of non-stationarity in stock dynamics.
  • Inclusion of Environmental Covariates: Tested for all stocks and adopted for black sea bass and yellowtail flounder. Finding models with covariates may not always “win” out.

Relevant Processes & Operations

Key management frameworks and scientific integration pathways:

  • Research Track Stock Assessments: Integrates climate impacts to improve short-term forecasts and reference points
  • Management Track Stock Assessments: Guides realistic, ecosystem-responsive rebuilding targets and harvest control rules
